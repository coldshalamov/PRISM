# A three-minute demo and skeptical judge answers

## Opening: 0:00–0:20

“Removing names is easy. Knowing what else you have exposed—and which scientific answers you have damaged—is harder. PRISM lets you inspect both before a dataset leaves your lab.”

Open the built-in fictional fixture. Say that it contains no real patients and its relationships are deliberately constructed for software testing.

## Default release: 0:20–0:55

Show the left table, right table, and exact 5-year / ZIP3 / 5kg recipe. Names are gone; ages and weights are intervals. Show the actual **75 of 480 unique correct matches** under the stated known-attributes/membership model. Explain that this is one modeled attacker, not a percent of the population guaranteed identifiable.

Show that all three declared analytical tasks remain within their tolerances. Good utility did not imply good identity protection.

## A useful repair: 0:55–1:35

Click Explore 12 alternatives. Select “Age 5 / ZIP 0 / kg 5.” Under this same fictional workload, verified unique matches fall to **20 of 480**, while the selected task answers do not change relative to the previous candidate. Explain why: those queries do not need geographic precision.

Do not call the candidate safe. The interface still says review required and exposes untested threats. The result demonstrates finding unnecessary disclosure rather than indiscriminately scrambling all columns.

## The surprise: 1:35–2:15

Open Attack lab. Pick one example depending on the judge:
- Two individually allowed aggregate sums: 5 minus 4 reveals an excluded participant's binary value.
- Two separately k=3 projections with linkable records can combine into k=1 in the constructed example.
- Exactly unchanged means and standard deviations can accompany a correlation flip from +1 to -1.

Each number is computed, not a video or an LLM-generated explanation. These are known failure classes, not newly discovered exploits. Their role is to make the reason for the controls immediately understandable.

## Close: 2:15–3:00

Open Methods & coverage and expand one limitation. Then show protected CSV export separately from the confidential owner audit.

“PRISM combines tested local calculations with a coverage register and a scientific workload. It does not say a dataset is 92% anonymous. It says what we tested, what changed, and what remains unknown. The point is to make a better release decision—not make a dangerous decision look official.”

## Answers worth having ready

**Is this new?**
“Not the privacy mathematics. ARX and other open-source tools already do serious work here. Our contribution is an accessible local review workflow, a consistent evidence model, and side-by-side scientific tradeoffs. We are building on prior art, not renaming it as a discovery.”

**Is a patient safe after your tool approves?**
“We do not issue that approval. Our outputs are bounded evidence under explicit assumptions. Institutional authority, independent privacy assessment, secure transmission, and untested threats remain separate.”

**Where is the AI?**
“The core does not need one. The calculations are local algorithms. Optional AI-assisted entity detection or synthetic generation can be integrated later, but we do not need an LLM to invent a risk score.”

**Why not just add noise?**
“Noise without a defined mechanism can destroy useful relationships and still leak under repeated or combined releases. Formal privacy requires accounting for the release mechanism and its outputs; cell noise alone is not that guarantee.”

**Does your high score detect all attacks?**
“There is no universal score. We report applicability and known gaps. The current exact matcher is deliberately narrow; membership, trajectory, genomic and independent external attacks are visibly unassessed.”

**Could I use this on an externally anonymized CSV?**
“Yes, for compatible structural and utility comparisons. Without trustworthy correspondence, it will not pretend it has verified record matches. Synthetic privacy additionally needs an appropriate control-data setup.”

**What runs already?**
“Thirteen transformations, capped k-suppression, structural and simulated linkage diagnostics, utility comparisons, twelve-candidate exploration in the UI, and three executable counterexamples. Optional library adapters are supplied but not runtime-verified here.”

## Rehearsal checklist

Use the provided fixtures and measured reports; disable network after installation; test export and cancellation; keep the default fixture report available if a browser interaction fails. Never improvise a clinical conclusion, a patent/novelty claim, a legal certification, or a numerical result not reproduced by the app.
